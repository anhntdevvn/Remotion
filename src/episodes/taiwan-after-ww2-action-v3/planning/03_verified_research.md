# 03 — Verified research

## Research question

Why was Japan unable to retain Taiwan after World War II, and why did the 1951 peace treaty renounce Japan's claim without naming a recipient?

## Verified chronology

| Date | Fact used in episode | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1895-04-17 | Under the Treaty of Shimonoseki, Qing China ceded Taiwan and the Penghu Islands to Japan. | High |
| 1943-12-01 | The Cairo communiqué stated that Formosa and the Pescadores should be restored to the Republic of China. | High |
| 1945-07-26 | The Potsdam Proclamation stated that the terms of the Cairo Declaration would be carried out. | High |
| 1945-09-02 | Japan's Instrument of Surrender accepted the Potsdam terms. | High |
| 1945 | General Order No. 1 directed Japanese forces in Formosa to surrender to Chiang Kai-shek. ROC authorities subsequently administered Taiwan. | High |
| 1946 | A U.S. State Department document said the transfer might still need formalization in an appropriate treaty. | High |
| 1947-02-28 | Conflict between local society and ROC administration escalated into the February 28 Incident and violent repression. | High |
| 1949 | The PRC was established on the mainland; the ROC government relocated to Taiwan. | High |
| 1950-01-05 | Truman stated that the United States would not provide military aid or advice to ROC forces on Taiwan at that time. | High |
| 1950-06-27 | After the outbreak of the Korean War, Truman ordered the Seventh Fleet to prevent an attack on Taiwan and also called on ROC forces to cease operations against the mainland. | High |
| 1951-09-08 | Article 2(b) of the San Francisco Peace Treaty stated that Japan renounced all right, title and claim to Formosa and the Pescadores. It did not name a recipient. | High |
| 1951 | Neither the ROC nor the PRC participated in the San Francisco conference. | High |

## Central analytical conclusion

The absence of a named recipient in Article 2(b) did not preserve Japan's claim. Japan had already accepted the Allied surrender framework and was under occupation. The treaty explicitly extinguished its right, title and claim. The ambiguity that remained concerned the disposition of Taiwan, not whether Japan could continue to possess it.

## Terms requiring careful wording

### `Transferred`, `restored`, `occupied`, `annexed`

Use these only with attribution or a precise event:

- The Cairo communiqué used the language of restoration to the Republic of China.
- General Order No. 1 designated who would receive the surrender of Japanese forces.
- ROC authorities began administering Taiwan in 1945.
- The final peace treaty in 1951 recorded Japan's renunciation without naming a recipient.

Avoid presenting a single contested legal interpretation as universally accepted fact.

### Present-day status

The episode does not adjudicate modern sovereignty claims. It explains the historical sequence that ended Japanese title and produced the treaty wording of 1951.

## Excluded or softened claims from the reference

- Do not say Taiwan was simply “given to China” without explaining the sequence of surrender administration and later treaty language.
- Do not claim that all Taiwanese preferred one government or another.
- Do not characterize Japan as entitled to keep Taiwan because it developed infrastructure there.
- Do not claim Mao consistently supported Taiwanese independence without a dedicated source and context.
- Do not frame the 1951 omission as proof of one present-day sovereignty position.
