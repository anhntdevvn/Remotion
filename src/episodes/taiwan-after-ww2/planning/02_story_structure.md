# 02 — Story structure

## Episode promise

The viewer will understand why Japan had no realistic route to retain Taiwan after 1945, why the United States later reconsidered Taiwan's strategic future, and why the 1951 treaty deliberately recorded Japan's renunciation without naming a recipient.

## Target runtime

- Planned duration: 10:40
- Acceptable production range after TTS: 9:45–11:45
- Narration: Korean
- Review text: Vietnamese
- Format: faceless animated documentary

## Retention design

### Hook

Open on the exact contradiction: Japan formally gave up Taiwan, but the treaty did not say to whom. Then ask why this ambiguity did not allow Japan to keep the island.

### Re-hooks

- 01:45 — “But this was not yet the treaty that ended the war.”
- 03:45 — “Receiving a surrender and receiving sovereignty are not the same sentence.”
- 05:35 — “Then China split into two governments.”
- 07:12 — “Five years after Japan's surrender, the Korean Peninsula changed the calculation.”
- 08:42 — “The final treaty solved Japan's claim, but left another question open.”

### Ending payoff

Japan lost Taiwan because the defeat removed its military and diplomatic power, it accepted the Allied surrender terms, and the peace treaty extinguished its claim. The later ambiguity concerned the recipient and final status, not a surviving Japanese right to retain Taiwan.

## Tone

- Curious and precise.
- Fast but not breathless.
- Neutral toward present-day sovereignty claims.
- Light visual humor only when it does not trivialize war, repression or civilian suffering.
- No patriotic framing for Japan, the ROC, the PRC or the United States.
