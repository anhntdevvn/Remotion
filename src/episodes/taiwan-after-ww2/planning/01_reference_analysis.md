## Central question

Why did Japan lose Taiwan after World War II even though the postwar settlement and Allied policy contained ambiguities about Taiwan's recipient and future status?

## What makes the reference effective

1. It opens with the postwar outcome, then introduces the apparent contradiction.
2. It compresses sixty years of history into a clear cause-and-effect chain.
3. Every narration beat receives a literal visual counterpart.
4. Maps explain territorial change; rooms and characters explain diplomacy.
5. Small signs and reaction shots release tension without stopping the story.
6. The animation is intentionally simple, allowing a high information density.

## Narrative grammar

- Question-first hook.
- Historical rewind to 1895.
- Rapid imperial timeline.
- Return to 1943–1945 decisions.
- Escalation through the Chinese Civil War.
- Three-policy-options sequence.
- Korean War as the decisive strategic turn.
- Treaty wording as the final reveal.

## Visual grammar

- Flat 2D character puppets.
- Reusable meeting-room and office backgrounds.
- Simplified regional maps with animated color changes.
- Flags and uniforms as fast identity cues.
- Placards and chalkboards for jokes and dense exposition.
- Mostly locked camera; motion comes from entrances, gestures, map fills and cuts.
- Minimal texture and shallow depth.

## Audio grammar

- Continuous, fast narration.
- Music remains subordinate to speech.
- Small impact, paper, stamp and map-transition sounds.
- Comedy is mostly visual and does not require exaggerated SFX.
- Few intentional pauses; the final treaty line deserves the longest pause.

## What the new episode retains

- The central historical question.
- The cause-and-effect progression from 1895 to 1951.
- A map/document/character visual language.
- Fast pacing and restrained humor.
- A final reveal centered on Article 2(b) of the San Francisco Peace Treaty.
